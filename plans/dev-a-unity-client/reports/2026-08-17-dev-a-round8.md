### A7 — can a player reach past ±2048 m?

## **YES.** Not theoretically — via the two helicopters in Dustbowl, and nothing stops it.

This is the one answer in this report I would ask you to read in full, because the failure mode is
not the one your question assumes.

**The chain, each link verified:**

1. `Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/LevelBounds.cs` exposes `public static bool IsInside(Vector3)`.
   **It has zero callers in all of `Assets/`.** The bounds are computed in `Awake` and then never
   consulted by anything. `LevelBounds` is documentation, not a constraint.
2. Dustbowl has 14 `VehicleSpawner`s, of which **two are helicopters** (`respawnType: 0`,
   `spawnTime: 16` — they respawn, so this is not a one-shot).
3. `Assembly-CSharp/Helicopter.cs` has **no altitude ceiling and no distance clamp**. Its only
   `Clamp` calls are `Clamp01` on rotor spin-up and on a autorotation dot product. Nothing reads
   `LevelBounds`.
4. `LevelBounds` on Dustbowl is 1700 × 700 × 1600 at (−70.8, 207.6, −88.6), so the play box edge is
   ~920 m out. From there a helicopter needs roughly another **1,100 m of level flight** to cross
   2048 m. At helicopter speed that is well under a minute, and there are ~1,900 backdrop transforms
   out there to fly toward.

**And the consequence is worse than the invisible wall in your question.** `Quantize.PackPos`
clamps rather than wraps:

```csharp
float t = Clamp01((v - POS_MIN) / POS_RANGE);   // POS_MAX 2048f, POS_RANGE 4096
```

So past 2048 m the wire value pins at the boundary while the *server's own simulation keeps
integrating the real position*. That means:

- **For the pilot:** every snapshot reports the clamped position, the real position keeps moving, and
  the delta grows past `PredictionReconciler`'s 0.25 m tolerance (four 6.25 cm wire steps) on
  **every single snapshot**. The reconciler adopts authority and replays — 20 times a second,
  forever. That is a permanent rubber-band, not a wall. It does not stop when you turn around,
  because the mismatch is in the encoding, not the movement.
- **For everyone else:** the actor freezes at the boundary and stays frozen, drawn at a place it
  is not.
- **It is silent.** Clamping is not an error, so no log fires and no counter moves. Someone reports
  "the helicopter broke" and nothing anywhere says why.

**What I would do, in preference order — your call, it is protocol:**

1. **Cheapest and it is already written:** call the existing `LevelBounds.IsInside` on the server,
   per actor, per tick, and refuse movement that leaves the box. One call site, uses code that
   already exists, no protocol change. It also fixes the pilot-walks-off-the-map case, not just the
   helicopter one.
2. **Make it loud:** have `PackPos` (or its caller) count clamps and expose the counter. Two lines,
   turns a silent corruption into a number. Worth doing even with (1), because (1) does not cover a
   route nobody has thought of yet.
3. Do not raise `POS_MAX`. It costs wire precision on every actor in every snapshot for a case that
   should be prevented rather than represented.

I would not ship the helicopters to a public match without at least (2).

### A12 — `cpuPercent`

**Leave it at −1.** Your reasoning holds and I could not name a better counter.

I checked the alternatives before answering rather than deferring to you: `Process.TotalProcessorTime`
is real on Windows and on Linux desktop, but the number a container reports is the *host's*
utilisation divided by nothing — under a cgroup CPU quota it reads as low while the server is
actually saturated, which is precisely the case matchmaking needs to detect. A wrong-by-construction
number on a matchmaking input is worse than an absent one, which is your argument, and it survives
contact with the specific counter.

**Two things about how the master consumes it, since you are asking about the field and not the
sender.** Both in `Ironfront.MasterServer/GameServers/GameServerRegistry.cs`:

1. `IsHealthy` (line 24) is safe with −1: `CpuPercent < 90f` passes trivially, and
   `AverageTickMs < 40f` — your real signal — does the actual gating. Good.
2. `Allocate` (line 83) is not: `if (best is null || server.CpuPercent < best.CpuPercent)`. With
   every server reporting −1, no comparison is ever true, so `best` is **whichever server
   `Dictionary` enumeration yields first** — stable-ish in practice, but load-blind by accident
   rather than by decision. It does not pile up, because `server.AssignedRoomId != 0` filters out
   an already-assigned server one line earlier, so the worst case is "new rooms prefer the same
   healthy server until it is busy" rather than "everything lands on one box".

**Recommendation, one line in your court:** sort `Allocate` on `AverageTickMs` — the signal you are
already sending and that is already real — and leave `cpuPercent` at −1 as an explicitly-absent
field. That makes the placement decision honest instead of accidental, and it needs no new counter
on my side.
